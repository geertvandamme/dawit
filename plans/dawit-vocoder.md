# DawIt Vocoder: play a voice as an instrument

Status: Released
Idea: [#121 — DawIt Vocoder: play a voice as an instrument](https://github.com/geertvandamme/dawit/issues/121)

## Goal
*DawIt Vocoder*, a built-in audio effect: the track it sits on is the **voice** (modulator); a carrier made inside it (a saw, pulse, choir or noise sound playing the song's chord, notes you pick or keys you play) is shaped by the voice's loudness in 4 to 24 bands. A beginner talks or sings and hears the words in a synth chord. A live picture shows voice, carrier and result band by band, and a guide explains bands, carriers, consonants and how a vocoder differs from pitch correction.

## What the app supports today (checked in code)
- **Audio input to an effect:** the track's stereo only. `Track::runPlugins` clears every channel beyond 2 ("side-chains start silent"); no feature feeds another track into an effect. So the carrier is made inside the effect; another track as carrier needs new routing (follow-up issue).
- **MIDI to an effect on an audio track:** `Track::process` passes live MIDI to every plugin in the chain of any non-bus track (`processBlock (view, midi)`), but `Engine::renderSegment` gives it only to the selected track, or, once any track is armed, to armed MIDI tracks. So keys (MIDI keyboard or the computer keyboard) reach a vocoder on the *selected* audio track when nothing is armed. Recorded MIDI clips only play on MIDI tracks, so notes cannot be recorded for it. Bounces have no live MIDI.
- **The song:** `SongContextReceiver` gives the key and the bar's chord as pitch-class masks (no root, so no inversions); a bar without a chord gives 0.
- **App only:** `dawit_add_builtin (... APP_ONLY)` (as Tune, Harmoniser, Arpeggiator) keeps it out of the VST3 and AU plugins, so there is no host, no side-chain bus and no AU MIDI question; `dawit_add_builtin` needs no new flag.
- **Name clash:** "vocoder" already appears in the app for DiffSinger's neural vocoder (*Sing this*); the guide says they differ.

(b) and (c) change render order, undo, saving, Signal Flow and the routing guide, and deserve their own plans; (a) already gives a playable carrier, and the song's chords make "sing over the progression" work today. File (b) and (c) as follow-up issues.
   Answer: (a). Track side-input routing and MIDI-track note routing are follow-up issues. With decision 2 there is no host side-chain either, so the sources are Song chord, Notes and Keys, and the effect has no side bus.
2. **Plugin build.** (a) VST3 and AU now (AU as `aumf` through a new MIDI-input flag on `dawit_add_builtin`); (b) `APP_ONLY`. **Recommended:** (a): the DSP needs no pitch tracker or PSOLA, so it builds cleanly; the issue asks to test standalone hosts. Whether a VST3 host sends MIDI to an effect varies by host (a check now left for the follow-up).
   Answer: (b), DawIt only for now. No MIDI-input flag on `dawit_add_builtin`, no `auval` or pluginval; a VST3/AU build is a follow-up issue.
3. **Theory Guides area.** *Effects* (with the other "What is X?" guides) or *Singing*. **Recommended:** *Effects*; the singing guide's *Harmonies and doubles* links to it.
   Answer: Effects.
4. **Consonants.** (a) Noise added to the top bands while the voice is hissy; (b) also pass the voice's own top end. **Recommended:** (a) alone: predictable, no voice leaking through; (b) goes to *Left for later*.
   Answer: (a), noise in the top bands.
5. **Band range.** Fixed 100 to 8,000 Hz (recommended: one less control, the guide's numbers stay simple) or adjustable.
   Answer: Adjustable: *Low* and *High* knobs (limits in decision 6).
6. **Range limits (new, follows from 5).** Low 50 to 400 Hz (default 100) and High 4,000 to 12,000 Hz (default 8,000). Low reaches the 1979 Moog vocoder's 50 Hz; High of at least 4 kHz keeps one band or more for the consonant noise; 12 kHz covers sibilants (up to about 10 kHz). The narrowest range, 400 to 4,000 Hz, is 3.32 octaves (telephone speech, 300 to 3,400 Hz, is still understood), so 24 bands stay 1.66 semitones wide. **Recommended:** these limits. Alternative: one *Range* choice (Voice, Wide, Telephone): simpler, less flexible.
   Answer: Use the recommended adjustable limits as the implementation choice for decision 5.

## Code
Recipes (`docs/recipes.md`): *A built-in effect or instrument* (copy DawIt Gate for the frame; no latency, so no `KeepsLatencyWhenBypassed`), *A theory guide* (via `fxui::Guide`), *Checking in the real app*.
- **Processor** `BuiltInVocoder` ("DawIt Vocoder", code `Dvoc`, registered `APP_ONLY`), implementing `SongContextReceiver`; stereo in and out only, no side bus; reads the live MIDI the chain passes (`acceptsMidi` true). Pure rules in `src/engine/Vocoder.{h,cpp}` (`vocoder::`, tested): band edges and centres from Bands, Low and High, Q, formant shift, chord voicing, consonant bands, starting points, test voice, advice. Its editor and guide keep the plugin rules (no app UI, no `Engine.h`), so a later plugin build is registration work.
- **DSP:** the voice is the mid (L+R)/2. Bands are log-spaced: edge k = Low × (High/Low)^(k/N), with High clamped to 0.45 × sample rate. Moving Bands, Low or High recomputes the coefficients and crossfades old and new banks over 20 ms; everything is sized for 24 bands in `prepareToPlay`, so the audio thread never allocates. Per band: two cascaded band-pass biquads for analysis, the same for synthesis, an envelope follower (attack 2 ms, release = Smoothing, RMS-style) scaled so a full-scale sine at the band centre reads 1. Carrier voices (max 16, band-limited saw or pulse, Choir = three saws detuned ±7 cents with independent left and right copies, Noise = independent noise per channel) are filtered by the synthesis bands, multiplied by the envelopes, summed. Consonants: noise into every band whose upper edge is above 4 kHz (High ≥ 4 kHz keeps at least one), gain = Consonants × how hissy the voice is, measured by two fixed detector filters on the voice that don't depend on the range (energy above 4 kHz against below 1.5 kHz, ramped over 10 ms). Formant moves the synthesis centres by n semitones, clamped below 0.45 × sample rate. Last stage: Carrier level, Mix, then a fixed soft limiter that keeps every sample within ±1.0, and a reset of filters on non-finite input.
- **Carrier source rule:** one chain, always ending at *Notes*: Chord with mask 0 or no song, Keys with nothing held (also while another track is armed, and in bounces) use Notes. Voices change notes with a 20 ms crossfade; a released key fades in 60 ms. Chord voicing: each pitch class at MIDI 48 + class (C3 = 130.81 Hz up), Range adds octaves above.
- **Threading:** parameters are atomics; the audio thread writes the band edges in force, per-band levels (voice, carrier, result) to atomics, the carrier's note mask, the fallback in force and an 8 s summary; the editor, guide and chip read them with timers and no lock. Hear-only band: atomic mask.
- **Plugin build:** none (`APP_ONLY`). Risk: the band-bank rebuild when Bands, Low or High move must stay click-free and allocation-free.

### Topic texts
**What a vocoder does** (Your voice shapes the colour of a synth chord.) A vocoder makes one sound speak with the words of another. You talk or sing into it. It measures how loud your voice is in many narrow slices of the spectrum, all the time, and uses those loudness values to shape a second sound, usually a synth chord. What you hear has the words of your voice and the notes of the synth. Say "ah" while the synth plays C, E and G and the chord opens like a mouth; say "s" and it hisses. Homer Dudley at Bell Labs began the idea in 1928 to send speech through less bandwidth: only the slow loudness values had to be sent, not the sound. In music the same values shape any sound you like. In DawIt Vocoder your track is the voice, and a carrier made inside the effect plays the notes.

**Modulator and carrier** (Which sound says the words and which one plays the notes.) The modulator is the sound whose movement you keep: your voice, or a drum loop. The carrier is the sound that gets shaped: a synth chord, or noise. The result has the carrier's pitch and the modulator's movement. In DawIt Vocoder the modulator is the track the effect sits on. The carrier is made inside, so you can play it from the song's chords, from notes you pick, or from a keyboard. What the vocoder keeps of your voice is its rhythm and the colour of its vowels and consonants. It does not keep your pitch.

**Bands** (How the voice is cut into slices and measured.) The vocoder cuts the voice into bands, spaced so that each is the same musical width. With 16 bands from 100 to 8,000 Hz each band is about 4.7 semitones wide, a ratio of 1.32: the lowest band is 100 to 131 Hz and the highest 6,083 to 8,000 Hz. Each band has a band-pass filter and an envelope follower, a meter that rises fast when the band gets louder and falls at the speed of Smoothing. The same filters are applied to the carrier, and each one is turned up and down by its meter. The sum is the result. Vowels are colours made of peaks called formants: in a typical man's voice "ah" has peaks near 730 and 1,090 Hz and "ee" near 270 and 2,290 Hz. The bands see those peaks and hand them to the carrier.

*Low* and *High* set where the bands start and end. The default, 100 to 8,000 Hz, holds a voice's vowels and most of its s sounds. A telephone passes only about 300 to 3,400 Hz and you still understand it, so a narrow range still carries words, and its bands are finer: 24 bands from 400 to 4,000 Hz are 1.7 semitones each. Lower Low towards 50 Hz for a deep voice or a drum loop; raise High towards 10,000 or 12,000 Hz when a bright voice's s sounds get lost. A wider range with the same number of bands makes each band wider.

**Why the carrier needs harmonics** (A sine has nothing for most bands to shape.) A band can only turn up what the carrier has in it. A sine wave at 110 Hz has energy at 110 Hz only, so with 16 bands just the one band that holds 110 Hz speaks, and you hear a hum. A sawtooth has a harmonic at every multiple of its pitch, each 1/n as loud as the first (the 2nd is 6 dB quieter, the 10th 20 dB quieter), so there is something to shape in every band. Noise has energy everywhere and no pitch, which is why a noise carrier gives a whisper. Even a saw leaves gaps: with a 110 Hz saw, bands below about 350 Hz are narrower than the 110 Hz between harmonics, so some hold none. That is why low bands can sound patchy, and why a chord of several saws fills the gaps better than one note.

**How many bands** (Four bands are readable; sixteen are clear.) More bands mean clearer words. In 1995 Shannon and colleagues found that listeners understood simple sentences from just three or four bands of noise, because the pattern of loudness over time already says a lot. Readable is not the same as clear: early vocoders had about 10 bands, and 10 to 15 is a common balance for speech. In DawIt Vocoder, 4 bands (edges at 100, 299, 894, 2,675 and 8,000 Hz) sound very robotic but you can follow the words; 8 bands are a robot with clear vowels; 12 are balanced; 16, the default, are clear; 24 are the most detailed, and need a rich carrier because each band is narrow.

**Consonants** (s and t are noise, so the carrier needs help.) Vowels are pitched, so their bands can follow them. Consonants such as s, f and t are mostly noise up high, around 4 to 8 kHz and sometimes up to 10 kHz (a rough rule: lower for men, higher for women). A synth has little energy there, so the "s" vanishes and words blur. Consonants adds noise to the bands above 4 kHz, only while your voice is hissy, so the noise follows the "s" and stays out of the vowels. Start near 40 to 50%. At 100% the hiss can sound harsh; at 0% words sound dull. If High is near 4,000 Hz, only the top band gets it. Other vocoders mix in some of the voice's own top end instead.

**Playing the carrier** (Song chord, chosen notes and keys, and what happens when one is missing.) *Song chord* plays the chord of the current bar, placed from C3 upwards: C major is C3 130.8 Hz, E3 164.8 Hz and G3 196.0 Hz, and Range adds octaves above. The song gives only the notes of the chord, not which one is lowest, so inversions are not followed. *Notes* plays the root and shape you choose. *Keys* plays what you play on a MIDI keyboard or the computer keyboard: select the track first, and note that while any track is armed for recording, keys go to that track instead. The keys you play are not recorded, and a bounce has none. Another track's sound cannot be the carrier yet. Whatever is missing (a bar with no chord, no key held), you hear your chosen *Notes* instead of silence.

**Vocoder or pitch correction?** (Your pitch is thrown away, not corrected.) Pitch correction, such as DawIt Tune, keeps your voice and moves its own pitch to the nearest note. A vocoder throws your pitch away: only the loudness of each band is used, so you can speak in a monotone and the chord still plays the notes. A harmoniser, such as DawIt Harmoniser, adds copies of your voice at other pitches. Three more things share the name: a talk box sends a synth through a tube into your mouth, so your mouth does the shaping; a phase vocoder is an FFT method for stretching time or shifting pitch; the neural vocoder in *Sing this* turns a spectrogram into sound. None of them is the same as this one.

**Using it well** (Give the bands a clear voice and a rich carrier.) The bands follow whatever is loudest, so use a close, dry, steady voice. Many engineers put a compressor before the vocoder so quiet syllables still move the bands, de-ess lightly if the "s" is piercing, and put reverb or delay after it, because the bands would measure the echoes; others vocode first and compress after, so take this as a start, not a rule. Keep the carrier in the song's key: Song chord does that for you. The result is usually quieter than the voice, so raise Carrier rather than the master; a soft limiter catches peaks above 0 dB. Mix below 100% lets some of the natural voice through (30 to 50% is a typical start).

### "Your track" rules
Computed from the 8 s summary (`vocoder::getAdvice`); the first that applies leads, others follow as separate sentences; quiet when none apply.
- No voice: input peak below −60 dBFS: "Nothing was heard from this track in the last 8 seconds. Play a part with a voice, or press Say "ah ee s"."
- Quiet voice: peak between −60 and −40 dB: "Your voice peaks at −46 dB. The bands can only give back what they hear: raise the track's level or compress it before the vocoder." (number from the summary)
- No chords: Song chord used the fallback over half of the time: "Song chord found no chord in most bars, so your chosen notes (C, E, G) played. Add chords on the chord track or choose Notes."
- Out of key: a chosen Notes note outside the song key (key known): "G♯ is not in A minor. Try the Minor shape on A."
- Dull words: voice hissy 5% of the time or more with Consonants below 20%: "Your voice has s and t sounds about 12% of the time, but Consonants is 0%. Try 40%."
- Low top: voice hissy 5% of the time or more with High below 6,000 Hz: "High is 4,500 Hz, so s sounds reach only the top band. Try 8,000 Hz." (number from the setting)
- Limiter: one or more limiter hits: "The soft limiter caught peaks. Lower Carrier by 3 dB."
- Four bands with a voice present: "With 4 bands the words are readable but robotic. Try 12 or 16 for clearer words."
- All fine: "Voice peaks at −14 dB, result at −9 dB: balanced. Try Soft choir for a pad." No key sentence when the song has no key.

### Changes
- **Mono is supported** (`isBusesLayoutSupported` accepts mono to mono as well as stereo to stereo), because the plugin registry test demands it of every effect. A mono buffer is heard as the same sound left and right (the one channel gets the right channel's result). The plan's "stereo in and out only" is otherwise unchanged: no side bus.
- **Envelope follower:** the plan's attack and release follower is fed by a quick symmetrical energy smoother per band (time constant of one period of the band's centre), otherwise a sine read about 25% too high because the fast attack and slow release act on every half cycle. Calibration: a sine at the band centre reads its amplitude (0.512 for 0.5). Attack and release are amplitude time constants (release = Smoothing: 36.8% after Smoothing; attack time constant 2 ms).
- **Attack test:** "63% within 2 ms" is measured on a high band (index 12, about 3.8 kHz): in a band near 800 Hz the band-pass filter itself needs about 5 ms to settle, so no follower could be faster. Measured 2.0 ms.
- **Range 2 test:** Range 2 only adds octaves, whose notes are harmonics of the lower ones on a saw, so a spectral test cannot tell them apart; the tests check the placement rule (`getChordNotes`) and the pitch-class mask instead.
- **Advice, out of key:** `getAdvice` takes `SongKey { mask, tonic, minor }`; with a key mask but no tonic it says "G♯ is not in the song's key. Try another root or shape." (the plan's text needs the tonic's name).
- **Level safety:** the soft limiter is skipped when Mix is 0, so Mix 0 is exactly the input. Limiter hits count samples whose pre-limit value passed ±1.0.
- **Known limit:** a key held when the track stops being the live track gets no note-off (the engine stops sending MIDI), so it stays held until the next note-off, All Notes Off or `prepareToPlay`. Group B1 can mention this or the engine can flush; not done.
